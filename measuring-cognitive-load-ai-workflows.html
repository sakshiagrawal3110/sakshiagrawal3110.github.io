Measuring Cognitive Load in AI-Augmented Workflows

Benchmarking task distribution with NASA-TLX, eye tracking, and behavioral metrics.

Role & scope

Role: Learner and co-member of the study
Timeline: 5 Months
Participants: 48 Participants
Research Type: Experimental Usability Study
Methods: Eye Tracking, NASA-TLX, Behavioral Timing, Cognitive Load Analysis

Overview

As AI copilots became embedded into professional workflows, stakeholders assumed automation inherently reduced user effort. Early observations suggested the opposite: poorly designed AI interactions often increased cognitive burden through verification overhead, context switching, ambiguity, and excessive recommendations.

This study investigated how AI assistance models influence cognitive load during complex workflow execution and when automation begins to harm rather than help.

Research objective

Which AI interaction models reduce cognitive load most effectively, and what balance between automation and agency optimizes performance?

Experimental design

Participants completed operational workflows across five AI-assisted conditions, from manual work to fully autonomous execution, while we recorded mental workload, attention, and performance.

Measurement framework

The study combined NASA-TLX, eye tracking, and behavioral metrics to capture both subjective load and objective attention patterns.

Key questions

  • Which AI models reduce cognitive load most effectively?
  • When does AI assistance become cognitively disruptive?
  • How does task delegation affect human attention patterns?
  • What balance between automation and agency optimizes performance?

AI conditions tested

  • Manual Workflow: no AI assistance.
  • Suggestive AI: recommendations surfaced but user retains control.
  • Guided AI: recommendations with concise reasoning and confidence indicators.
  • Conversational AI: interactive copilot support with dialogue-driven tasks.
  • Autonomous AI: the system executes actions automatically.

Measurement dimensions

  • NASA-TLX: mental demand, temporal demand, effort, frustration, perceived performance.
  • Eye Tracking: fixation duration, gaze switching, scan complexity, verification loops, attention fragmentation.
  • Behavioral Metrics: completion speed, correction frequency, hesitation time, override behavior, trust confidence.

Major findings

  • Excessive recommendations increased cognitive fatigue: persistent AI suggestions caused attention fragmentation, decision fatigue, and reduced focus retention. High-frequency recommendation interfaces increased gaze switching by 42%.
  • Guided explainability reduced cognitive stress: users performed best when AI explained reasoning concisely, surfaced confidence indicators, and reduced ambiguity. Guided AI reduced NASA-TLX cognitive load scores by 32%.
  • Full autonomy reduced situational awareness: autonomous systems improved speed but lowered task understanding, confidence, and accountability awareness. Situational awareness dropped 28% in autonomous conditions.
  • Conversational AI improved learnability but increased time cost: participants appreciated flexibility, but interaction overhead, prompt formulation burden, and conversational inefficiency increased task time.

Quantitative outcomes

  • Participants: 48
  • Workflow conditions tested: 5
  • Eye-tracking sessions: 240
  • Cognitive load reduction: 32%
  • Gaze switching increase (high AI density): 42%
  • Situational awareness decline (autonomy): 28%
  • Task completion improvement: 24%

Design implications

  • AI should reduce decisions, not add more decisions: poor recommendation density increases mental strain.
  • Explainability must be lightweight: long explanations create additional cognitive burden.
  • Visibility preserves human oversight: users require awareness even when automation is efficient.

Final reflection

This study reinforced that AI UX success cannot be measured solely through efficiency metrics. Cognitive sustainability, attentional clarity, and mental effort are equally critical dimensions of experience quality. The research fundamentally shaped my approach to designing AI-assisted workflows that feel supportive rather than mentally exhausting.
