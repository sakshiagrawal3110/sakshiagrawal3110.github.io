Case Study

AI Copilot for Enterprise SaaS

Designing an AI-powered in-product assistant for enterprise teams.

Enterprise users were spending excessive time navigating fragmented workflows, manually searching documentation, and switching between tools to complete routine operational tasks. I led the end-to-end UX strategy, research, and interaction design for an AI-powered in-product copilot that leveraged contextual retrieval, workflow automation, and natural language interactions to help enterprise users complete tasks faster.

The final solution reduced average task completion time by 40%, increased feature discoverability by 63%, and achieved a 71% weekly engagement rate within three months of launch.

Overview

Problem Statement

  • High cognitive overload caused by fragmented navigation structures.
  • Repetitive multi-step workflows requiring 8–15 clicks.
  • Low discoverability of advanced features.
  • Dependence on customer support and documentation.
  • Context switching between dashboards, spreadsheets, and knowledge bases.
  • Low confidence while performing high-risk operational tasks.

Business Goals

  • Understand user intent in natural language.
  • Provide contextual assistance within workflows.
  • Automate repetitive tasks.
  • Reduce training and onboarding costs.
  • Improve operational efficiency at scale.
Research Phase

Research Objectives

We wanted to understand how enterprise users currently complete workflows, where friction and hesitation occur, trust perceptions around AI systems, mental models around automation and recommendations, and behavioral differences between novice and expert users.

Quantitative Product Analytics

  • Session recordings
  • Funnel drop-offs
  • Feature adoption metrics
  • Clickstream behavior
  • Workflow completion rates
  • Search query logs

Contextual User Interviews

Conducted 24 interviews across operations managers, customer success teams, financial analysts, enterprise administrators, and product operations teams.

  • Users valued speed over feature depth.
  • Most users wanted fewer decisions, not more features.
  • Users distrusted “black-box automation”.
  • Users preferred assistive AI over autonomous AI.
  • Users expected AI to remember context and explain recommendations.

Diary Study

A 10-day diary study with 12 participants revealed repetitive task fatigue, workaround creation using spreadsheets, frequent interruptions during workflows, and high dependency on tribal knowledge. This surfaced the high-frequency workflow clusters ideal for AI augmentation.

Defining the AI Opportunity
  • Navigation FrictionToo many clicks → Natural language shortcuts
  • Knowledge RetrievalDifficult documentation → Context-aware semantic search
  • Workflow RepetitionManual repetitive actions → Suggested automation
  • Error PreventionFear of mistakes → AI-generated guidance
  • Feature DiscoveryHidden capabilities → Intelligent recommendations
Design Strategy

Principles

  • AI Should Feel Assistive, Not Autonomous: Users needed to remain in control.
  • Context Over Conversation: The assistant should understand current screen, user role, workflow state, historical actions, and permissions.
  • Progressive Trust Building: Start with recommendations, explain reasoning, and request confirmation for complex automation.
  • Reduce Cognitive Load: Use predictive suggestions, inline actions, contextual prompts, and lightweight patterns.

UX Exploration

  • Option A — Traditional Chatbot: Rejected because it felt detached from workflows.
  • Option B — Command Palette Style Assistant: Fast but too technical for non-power users.
  • Option C — Embedded Contextual Copilot: Selected for conversational flexibility, contextual awareness, and workflow integration.

Usability testing showed a 31% higher success rate compared to the chatbot concept.

Key UX Features

Contextual AI Panel

The assistant dynamically adapted to page context, user permissions, task state, and historical usage patterns to reduce repeated explanations.

Natural Language Task Execution

Users could type commands like “Generate monthly report for APAC accounts” and the AI translated intent into executable system actions.

Explainability Layer

Every recommendation included why it appeared, confidence indicators, data sources, and editable parameters to improve trust.

AI-Powered Workflow Recommendations

Proactive suggestions surfaced shortcuts, automations, next steps, and frequently paired workflows personalized by behavioral clustering.

Inline Learning & Onboarding

Contextual guidance was embedded directly in workflows, reducing reliance on training teams and external documentation.

Prototyping & Testing

Prototype Fidelity

I created low-fidelity workflow maps, conversational interaction flows, high-fidelity Figma prototypes, and simulated AI response scenarios to validate the model and edge cases.

Round 1

  • Tested discoverability, prompt comprehension, trust perception, and conversational expectations.
  • Found users preferred suggested prompts over blank states.
  • AI confidence scores improved trust perception by 26%.
  • Users wanted editable AI outputs before execution.

Round 2

  • Focused on workflow efficiency, learnability, repeat usage, and enterprise scalability.
  • Task completion time reduced by 40%.
  • User confidence improved by 34%.
  • Workflow abandonment reduced by 29%.
  • Time-to-first-success improved from 14 minutes to 5 minutes.
Accessibility & Responsible AI
  • Transparency: users could inspect recommendations, edit outputs, reject suggestions, and provide feedback.
  • Error Recovery: fallback handling for ambiguous prompts, failed retrievals, permissions conflicts, and hallucination risks.
  • Accessibility: keyboard navigation, screen readers, low cognitive load patterns, and clear semantic hierarchy.

Launch Strategy

  • Phase 1: AI assistance for onboarding and search.
  • Phase 2: Workflow generation and recommendations.
  • Phase 3: Predictive automation and proactive suggestions.
  • Staged rollout allowed safe feedback collection, model retraining, trust monitoring, and enterprise risk reduction.

Final Reflection

This project transformed how I think about AI-assisted product experiences. Designing for enterprise AI required balancing automation with control, efficiency with transparency, intelligence with predictability, and scalability with usability.

The result was an AI system that augmented human workflows rather than attempting to replace them.
