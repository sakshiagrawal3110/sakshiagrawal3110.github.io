Case Study

Mental Models Research for Generative AI

Understanding how non-technical users form trust in AI systems.

As generative AI adoption accelerated, the company needed deeper insight into how non-technical users understood, trusted, and interacted with AI systems.

I led a mixed-methods research initiative investigating how users form mental models around generative AI, including perceived intelligence, trust signals, correctness evaluation, anthropomorphism, and explainability effects.

Project Overview
RoleUX Research Lead
Timeline5 Months
Research TypeMixed Methods Research
Participants68 Participants Across 5 Demographics
Focus AreasGenerative AI, Mental Models, Trust, Human-AI Interaction
Overview

Context

The research explored how users with limited technical AI knowledge perceive generative systems, what builds trust, and how product design can support healthy skepticism.

Research Focus

  • How users perceive AI intelligence
  • What influences trust and skepticism
  • How users evaluate correctness
  • How anthropomorphism affects behavior
  • How explainability changes interaction patterns
Research Goals

Study objectives

  • How do non-technical users conceptualize generative AI?
  • What factors influence trust formation?
  • How do users respond to incorrect AI outputs?
  • How do conversational patterns affect perceived intelligence?
  • What UX interventions improve healthy trust calibration?
Methodology

Mixed-methods framework

  • Quantitative surveys
  • Moderated interviews
  • Behavioral observation
  • Usability testing
  • Diary studies
  • Thematic analysis

Participant demographics

  • Students
  • Operations professionals
  • Marketers
  • Business analysts
  • Administrative workers

Most participants had limited technical understanding of AI systems.

Research Phases

Phase 1 — Baseline Perception Survey

A large-scale survey explored perceived AI intelligence, trust levels, expectations, emotional reactions, and usage behaviors.

Phase 2 — Behavioral Observation

Participants completed tasks using generative AI tools while I observed prompting behavior, trust repair patterns, verification tendencies, frustration triggers, and emotional reactions.

Findings

Key survey insights

  • 61% believed AI “understands” information like humans.
  • 47% assumed confident outputs were accurate.
  • 58% struggled to identify hallucinated responses.
  • 72% expected conversational memory even when unavailable.

Behavioral patterns

  • Users anthropomorphized AI heavily.
  • Confidence signals influenced trust more than accuracy.
  • Verification was rare until mistakes appeared.
  • Explainability improved healthy trust calibration.
Framework Development

Mental Model Maturity Framework

  • Stage 1 — Anthropomorphic Trust
    Users perceive AI as human-like.
  • Stage 2 — Functional Assistance
    Users begin understanding task boundaries.
  • Stage 3 — Critical Collaboration
    Users treat AI as an assistive probabilistic system.

Outcome

The framework became foundational for internal AI UX guidelines and influenced onboarding, transparency, and trust calibration patterns.

Design Implications

Trust and transparency

  • Interfaces should communicate uncertainty, confidence, source reliability, and limitations.
  • Conversational fluency can create false confidence.
  • UX must support verification through fact checking and editable outputs.

Interaction guidelines

  • Source visibility and confidence indicators help build healthy trust.
  • Structured outputs and explanation summaries improve calibration.
  • Collaborative review patterns reduce misuse risk.
Deliverables
  • Enterprise AI trust guidelines
  • Explainability UX patterns
  • AI onboarding recommendations
  • Transparency frameworks
  • Interaction design principles
  • Trust calibration heuristics

Strategic Learnings
  • AI UX is fundamentally psychological — perception often matters as much as technical capability.
  • Explainability is a UX problem, not just an engineering one.
  • Healthy skepticism improves long-term trust.
Final Reflection

This project deepened my understanding of human-AI interaction and reinforced the importance of designing AI systems that are understandable, trustworthy, and ethically responsible.

The research ultimately influenced both product direction and organizational AI design principles.
